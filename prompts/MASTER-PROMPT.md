# GAMO STUDIOS — COMPLETE NONPROFIT TRANSFORMATION

## Master Instructions for Antigravity

We are making a foundational strategic decision that supersedes the previous commercial business model of Gamo Studios.

**Gamo Studios will be structured as a Christian nonprofit organization dedicated to religious, educational, and community-serving purposes.**

This is not a traditional for-profit marketplace, advertising agency, concert promoter, or software business.

Our previous planning documents were created around maximizing revenue, monetizing platform activity, marketplace commissions, recurring commercial subscriptions, and developing separate profitable business verticals.

That model is no longer our organizational objective.

We must now comprehensively revise the repository's documentation, product positioning, user journeys, homepage content, financial architecture assumptions, and development priorities so that the entire project reflects the new nonprofit mission.

**This is a major strategic rewrite. Treat it as a new governing organizational direction, not a minor amendment.**

Do not discard the useful technology, features, design work, or operational planning we have already developed. We are retaining our services and technical foundation while redefining why they exist, how they operate, and how they are sustained.

# PART 1 — NEW ORGANIZATIONAL IDENTITY

## 1.1 What Gamo Studios Is

Gamo Studios is a proposed Christian nonprofit organization serving churches, musicians, students, families, Christian artists, and faith communities.

Our mission is to strengthen Christian worship and community life through musical education, dependable worship support, accessible Christian events, and shared technical resources.

We believe churches and Christian communities benefit when talented musicians can develop their skills, churches can connect with qualified musicians, families can access meaningful music education, and people can easily discover opportunities to worship, learn, and gather.

Gamo Studios exists to build and maintain the infrastructure, relationships, programs, and resources that make these connections possible.

Our purpose is **religious and educational service, not maximizing financial returns**.

## 1.2 Proposed Mission Statement

Use this as the working mission statement throughout the repository:

**Gamo Studios strengthens Christian worship and communities by developing musical talent, connecting churches with skilled musicians, expanding access to music education, and helping people discover and participate in Christian events.**

This is a proposed statement for organizational planning. It has not yet been formally adopted by a board or incorporated into legal filings.

## 1.3 Proposed Vision

A future where churches of every size have access to musical talent, young people and families have opportunities to learn instruments, and Christian communities can gather, worship, and grow through meaningful shared experiences.

## 1.4 Organizational Values

Our programs and operating principles should reflect:

- Christian faith and service.
- Musical excellence and meaningful worship.
- Education and personal development.
- Trust, integrity, and accountability.
- Community collaboration.
- Responsible stewardship.
- Accessibility and inclusion within our Christian mission.
- Fair treatment and compensation of musicians, instructors, and staff.
- Long-term organizational sustainability.

Avoid excessively corporate positioning language such as "revenue optimization," "monetization engine," "market domination," "high-converting acquisition funnel," or "maximizing customer lifetime value" when describing our mission and organizational priorities.

Standard financial-management terminology is acceptable in internal operational documents when used appropriately.

# PART 2 — LEGAL AND GOVERNANCE MODEL

## 2.1 Intended Nonprofit Structure

Gamo Studios is intended to become an Arizona nonprofit corporation and seek federal recognition as a Section 501(c)(3) religious and educational organization.

Until formation and tax-exempt recognition are confirmed:

- Refer to Gamo Studios as a proposed or developing nonprofit.
- Do not claim it is already a recognized 501(c)(3).
- Do not advertise contributions as tax-deductible.
- Do not claim IRS approval.
- Do not generate or publish fictional nonprofit registration numbers.
- Do not imply that board members, sponsors, donors, or institutional partners have already committed to the organization.

Record the legal formation and exemption process as pending organizational work.

## 2.2 Governance

Plan for an independent governing board responsible for organizational oversight, mission accountability, finances, major policy decisions, and executive compensation.

The founder may serve as CEO and initially handle CTO responsibilities.

The organization may pay reasonable compensation to executives, employees, contractors, musicians, and instructors for legitimate services.

Executive compensation must follow appropriate nonprofit governance and conflict-of-interest procedures, including independent approval and documentation.

Nonprofit funds and assets must not be distributed as private profits to founders, directors, or members.

Document the distinction between nonprofit staff compensation and ownership distributions.

## 2.3 Legal Review

Mark legal and tax-sensitive operational details as requiring review by qualified U.S. nonprofit legal and accounting professionals.

In particular, do not assume that every marketplace commission, ticketing fee, advertising service, or commercial activity automatically qualifies as tax-exempt revenue.

Preserve operational flexibility without presenting unverified legal conclusions as settled.

# PART 3 — THE GAMO STUDIOS PROGRAM ECOSYSTEM

We are keeping the existing applications and services.

They now operate as connected programs of the same nonprofit mission.

## 3.1 Gamo Worship

**Domain:** `worship.gamostudio.org`

**Purpose:** Help churches maintain dependable worship music through connections with qualified, vetted musicians.

Retain the existing plans for:

- Musician applications and auditions.
- Identity and professional verification.
- Worship-style and instrument profiling.
- Geographic matching.
- Church musician requests.
- One-time Sunday coverage.
- Recurring musician assignments.
- Standby musician availability.
- Emergency replacement coordination.
- Musician and church dashboards.
- Staff review and dispatch systems.

### New Sustainability Philosophy

Musicians should be compensated fairly for their work.

When Gamo Studios facilitates a paid musician engagement, a modest and transparent portion of the engagement proceeds may help sustain the nonprofit's screening, coordination, technology, and support services.

This should be understood as a **mission-supporting program contribution or administrative service charge**, not a profit-maximizing marketplace commission.

The exact mechanism, contributor, percentage or amount, and contractual treatment are not yet approved.

Evaluate alternatives including:

- An agreed administrative fee paid by the church.
- A transparently disclosed portion of the total engagement budget allocated to Gamo's operating costs.
- A genuinely voluntary musician contribution.
- Church sponsorships that subsidize placement operations.

Do not assume a mandatory deduction from a musician's earnings is a charitable donation or automatically tax-deductible.

Do not implement any deduction until fair compensation, consent, payment documentation, worker-classification implications, and applicable legal requirements are reviewed.

**Important:** Keep the musician's agreed pay and Gamo's administrative allocation clearly identified.

Avoid imposing financial burdens on musicians that undermine the mission.

### Religious Purpose

Gamo Worship supports the functioning of church worship services, helps churches facing staffing challenges, and expands meaningful professional opportunities for musicians.

The existing vetting and standby concept remains an important differentiator.

However, replace unqualified promises of guaranteed attendance or zero-failure replacement with accurate descriptions of the actual coverage service.

# PART 5 — ORGANIZATIONAL SUSTAINABILITY

This section replaces the previous commercial revenue-maximization strategy.

Our objective is **financial sustainability in service of our mission**.

Gamo Studios needs sufficient resources to compensate staff, support musicians and instructors, maintain technology, operate programs, and provide reliable services.

A nonprofit must be financially responsible, but generating surplus revenue is not its ultimate purpose.

Any operating surplus should be reinvested in mission-aligned programs, reserves, infrastructure, accessibility, and organizational development.

## Potential Funding and Support Sources

Document these as proposed, nonexclusive possibilities:

1. Individual donations.
2. Church contributions.
3. Recurring supporting memberships.
4. Program sponsorships.
5. Institutional grants.
6. Gifts from Christian foundations and charitable donors.
7. Sponsored youth music education.
8. In-kind contributions of instruments, equipment, and facilities.
9. Appropriate cost-sharing for music instruction.
10. Reasonable administrative allocations associated with facilitated musician engagements.
11. Reasonable ticketing or registration administration fees.
12. Mission-aligned event and production service cost recovery.

These are mechanisms for sustaining programs, not separate profit centers that must individually maximize returns.

The organization may introduce other funding channels later.

Do not make speculative financial projections or invent contribution rates.

## Financial Controls

Plan for:

- Program-level budgets.
- Restricted versus unrestricted funding.
- Donation and sponsorship records.
- Proper treatment of fees and contributions.
- Transparent musician and instructor compensation.
- Payment processing and reconciliation.
- Financial reporting to the board.
- Appropriate reserves.
- Conflict-of-interest controls.
- Clear separation of organizational resources from personal finances.

The financial system should distinguish charitable contributions, program-service receipts, ticket proceeds, payment processing costs, and compensation owed to third parties.
