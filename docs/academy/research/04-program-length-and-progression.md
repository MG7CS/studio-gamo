# Gamo Academy research 04: program length and progression

Date: 2026-11-16. Scope: how long a Gamo Academy program should run, and when a student is ready to take an instrument home and attend class less often so hosted-site space and instruments can serve new students. Ideal first, constrained trade-offs last.

Evidence labels used throughout:

- **[S]** read in a source this session (page, PDF or paper opened and read).
- **[S†]** seen only in a search-tool summary of a source, not opened in full. Treat as one step weaker than [S].
- **[K]** prior knowledge or my own judgment. Unverified unless a source is named. Numbers marked *(unverified)* must be checked before they appear anywhere public.
- **[M]** a number produced by my own simple model from stated assumptions (not a finding).

Gamo Academy rules respected: no tuition, fee or pay recommendations are made here. All prices are market reference points only. Any lending program, vendor partnership or money flow is an owner decision (see Open questions).

## Summary

- **Published exam hours give a usable design anchor.** Trinity College London's regulated figures are 40 total qualification hours (TQT) for Initial, 60 for Grade 1, 90 for Grade 2, 120 for Grade 3 and 150 for Grade 4. Only 8, 12, 18, 18 and 24 of those hours are teacher-led. The rest (80% at Initial through Grade 2) is independent practice. [S] I found no instrument-specific hours for guitar, drums or voice, so these figures are the best proxy for all five Gamo instruments.
- **Students need roughly 2 hours of practice a week by Grade 1 to 3, and nearly 3 hours at Grade 4.** In a 2,559-student UK sample, self-reported weekly practice averaged 116 minutes at Grade 1, 132 at Grade 2, 140 at Grade 3 and 192 at Grade 4. [S] Class time alone cannot supply that, so home practice is the plan, not a bonus.
- **Recommended program shape:** a 12-week term, three terms a year (36 teaching weeks), and a full-year first commitment. Ofsted-endorsed whole-class programs recommend a tuition phase of at least one year [S], and a strong pathway (planned progression, small-group follow-on) kept 50 to 70% of children learning versus about one third nationally. [S]
- **Graduation of independence (gradual release of responsibility):** Phase A supported, Phase B blended, Phase C independent. Default for piano: 16 weeks in A, 20 weeks in B, then monthly masterclass. Guitar and bass: 12 and 24. Drums: 16 and 20 (pad practice at home from week 1, kit stays on site). Voice: 8 and 28 (nothing to take home). By week 36 a student has about 90 to 105 total learning hours, close to Initial plus Grade 1 (100 hours cumulative if the Trinity figures are treated as additive; [K] assumption). [M]
- **Take-home readiness is a gate, not a date.** Time sets the earliest, typical and latest exit; a five-part Home-Ready Check (skills, habit, care, home setup, own motivation) decides. Practice-log consistency and parent commitment are the two factors the research supports most strongly. [S] The specific thresholds are my judgment [K] and should be tested in a pilot.
- **Capacity gain is large.** Worked example: a site with 8 instruments, open 20 hours a week at 80% utilization, serves about 54 new students a year (3 intakes of 18) when students graduate through the phases, versus a roster of about 73 total if every student keeps attending class plus one supervised practice forever. After three years that is a roster near 160 learners on the same 8 instruments. [M]
- **The real new constraint moves to home instruments.** About 108 students at a time sit in Phase C at steady state in the example. Each needs a home instrument. Entry-level weighted digital pianos run about $400 to $500 new [S], so renting commercially costs more than buying after about 6 months. A Gamo-owned lending pool, sized by formula in section 5, is the likely answer for families who cannot buy (owner decision).
- **Engagement after leaving the site must be designed.** Research ties continuing to felt competence, relatedness and autonomy [S], to autonomy-supportive teaching [S] and to parent involvement [S†]. The plan: termly showcases and progress reviews, an ensemble day each term, monthly masterclass, short video check-ins, practice logs, and a safety-net "step back" to Phase B.

Published sources give hours to a grade but not hours to "first songs." The following are my estimates [K] *(unverified)*, to be replaced by Gamo pilot data:

## 2. Readiness criteria and the graduation of independence

### 2.1 Model

The phases follow the gradual release of responsibility model: the teacher models ("I do"), then guides ("we do"), then students work together ("you do it together"), then alone ("you do it alone"), with support removed as skill grows. Pearson and Gallagher named the model in 1983 and drew on Vygotsky's scaffolding; Fisher and Frey added the four-phase wording. [S] Wikipedia article; [S†] Emerald chapter by Pearson, McVee and Shanahan (2019). The Suzuki approach, the most widely copied early-music model, states the same aim: parent as "home teacher" at first, with involvement that "usually lessens over time" and the child eventually practicing alone. [S]

Mapping to Gamo:

| Phase | Name | Gradual release stage | Where practice happens | Teacher role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Supported | I do, we do | On site, supervised, plus class | Models, corrects, sets the practice routine |
| B | Blended | You do together | Mostly home; open on-site practice optional; ensemble work | Checks, coaches, adjusts targets |
| C | Independent | You do alone | At home | Observes monthly, sets next goals |

### 2.2 Home-Ready Check (recommended gate)

Time sets when to *look*; the check decides. All thresholds are [K] judgment, to be tested in a pilot (the research supports the factors, not these cutoffs).

Five gates; a student passes with Gates 1 and 2 required and at least 4 of 5 overall:

1. **Skills** (instrument checklist below), shown live in a 15-minute check with the teacher.
2. **Habit.** Attendance at 80% or more of classes and supervised sessions in the last 8 weeks (about 10 of 12). Practice log shows 4 or more days a week in at least 6 of the last 8 weeks, at the age-appropriate length (10 to 15 minutes under age 9; 20 minutes ages 9 to 12; 25 to 30 minutes 13 and up, consistent with the Suzuki guidance that practice length grows with attention span [S]). Teachers spot-check logs by asking the student to play what the log claims. Practice diaries are the most common tracking tool among UK teachers (67%) [S], and the same report finds "lack of practice and motivation" is the top barrier to progress (63%) [S], so verifying beats trusting.
3. **Care.** Student can set up, tune (or check), carry and store the instrument safely; family has signed the home agreement (care, supervised use, return terms for any lent instrument).
4. **Home.** A named adult practice supporter; a quiet, regular spot and time; the instrument, stand or bench and headphones (see section 3).
5. **Own motivation.** A short conversation in which the student names a personal goal (a song, a worship team, a family event). This is where autonomy support starts; see section 4.

Why parent commitment is a gate: parents of children who stopped lessons were less interested in music and did not increase involvement; parents of highly skilled young musicians tended to attend lessons and interact with the teacher (Davidson, Howe, Moore, Sloboda 1996) [S†]. Parental involvement here means attention and presence, not musical skill.

### 2.3 Skills checklist per instrument

All items [K]. A student shows items as playing, not describing.

| Instrument | Skills checklist for take-home readiness |
|---|---|
| Piano/keyboard | Sits and positions hands without prompting. Plays 3 short pieces from notation (or chord chart) with both hands together at a steady 60 to 80 beats per minute. Reads notes within a 5-finger position in both clefs. Plays a one-octave C major scale hands separately and hands together slowly. Uses the sustain pedal on cue. |
| Acoustic or electric guitar | Tunes with a clip-on tuner. Holds the guitar and picks or strums without help. Plays at least 4 of G, C, D, Em, Am cleanly and changes between two of them at about 60 beats per minute. Plays 3 songs with a steady strum. Reads a chord chart or tab line. |
| Bass | Tunes with a tuner. Plays root-and-fifth lines on 3 songs with the click. Mutes unused strings. Reads a simple chart. |
| Drums/percussion | Correct stick grip and posture. Plays an 8th-note rock beat (kick, snare, hi-hat) for one minute at 70 to 90 beats per minute with a metronome. Plays single strokes, double strokes and a paradiddle slowly on a pad. Reads a simple one-line rhythm. |
| Voice | Sings 3 songs in tune from memory with a steady pulse. Comfortable range of about an octave. Uses relaxed posture and breath. Checks own pitch against a recording. Knows warm-up and rest rules (no pushing, no long loud sessions). |

### 2.4 Recommended phases, weeks and hours by instrument

Assumptions [M]: class 45 minutes; supervised practice 60 minutes; a student keeps the same hosted-site contact rules in all phases. Supervised practice counts as independent learning in Trinity's sense (not teacher-led).

| | Piano/keyboard | Guitar and bass | Drums/percussion | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase A weeks** (earliest / typical / latest) | 12 / 16 / 24 | 8 / 12 / 16 | 12 / 16 / 20 | 4 / 8 / 12 |
| A class | weekly, 45 min | weekly, 45 min | weekly, 45 min | weekly, 45 min |
| A supervised practice | 2 a week in the first half of A, 1 a week in the second half | same | same (on-site kit); pad, sticks and metronome at home from week 1 | 1 a week (coaching with accompaniment tracks) |
| A hours: class + supervised (+ home) | 12 + 24 (+0) = 36 | 9 + 18 (+4 trial loan) = 31 | 12 + 24 (+16 pad) = 52 | 6 + 8 (+8 recordings) = 22 |
| **Phase B weeks** | 20 | 24 | 20 | 28 |
| B class | weekly for 8 weeks, then every other week (14 sessions) | weekly 8, then biweekly (16 sessions) | same as piano (14) | weekly 8, then biweekly (18) |
| B open on-site practice | 1 hour every other week (10 h) | 12 h | 10 h (kit) | 14 h (accompanist or practice room) |
| B home practice | about 100 min a week | same | same (pad, then electronic kit if space allows) | same |
| B hours: class + open + home | 10.5 + 10 + 33 = 54 | 12 + 12 + 40 = 64 | 10.5 + 10 + 33 = 54 | 13.5 + 14 + 47 = 74 |
| **Total by week 36 (Levels 0 and 1)** | about 90 | about 95 | about 106 | about 96 |
| **Phase C** (from week 37) | monthly 60-min masterclass, one ensemble day a term, a short video review a month | same | same, plus a monthly on-site kit slot | same |
| C home practice | about 120 min a week or more | same | same | same |

Frequency summary (all instruments):

- **Phase A:** 3 on-site contacts a week at the start (class plus 2 supervised practices), 2 later. About 2.75 on-site hours a week early, 1.75 later (voice 1.75 throughout).
- **Phase B:** class weekly for 8 weeks then every other week; open practice every other week; home practice 5 days a week of 15 to 25 minutes (about 100 minutes a week). About 1 on-site hour a week on average.
- **Phase C:** one masterclass or check-in a month (60 minutes), one ensemble day a term, 5 to 6 home days a week.

Why these weeks:

- Piano gets the longest A because a weighted digital piano is the costliest home item and the one most families need time (and sometimes a loan) to arrange. The NIU Suzuki program also insists on a piano rather than a keyboard. [S]
- Guitar and bass can leave A sooner: instruments are cheap and portable, and a 4-week trial loan in weeks 9 to 12 lets the student prove the habit before B. [K]
- Drums leave A earliest *for the pad* (week 1) but never fully leave the site for the kit: noise, size and cost make a real kit a poor home loan. Merriam Music notes electronic kits are quieter for practice and a metronome (phone app or device) is recommended. [S]
- Voice has no instrument to lend, so home practice starts in week 1 with recordings; on-site time is for coaching. [K]
- Young children (under about 8) add 4 to 8 weeks to A and need a parent present in every phase (Suzuki, Yamaha both require it for early courses). [S] The age rule is [K].

**How other programs decide when a student gets an instrument** (what I could verify):

## 4. Keeping students engaged after they leave on-site practice

### 4.1 Evidence

- **Why students stop:** the 2014 UK Making Music report (ABRSM with partners): children stop playing by 11.1 years on average (of the 16% who stopped); main reasons were lack of interest and competing pressures, with cost, no school opportunity and lack of an own instrument also listed; teachers' top barriers to progress were lack of practice and motivation (63%) and unsupportive family (29%). [S]
- **Continuation is fragile without a plan:** only just under a third of UK pupils continued to learn an instrument after whole-class tuition; one program with planned progression (band plus small-group lessons) saw 50% to 70% transition. [S]
- **Psychological needs:** dropout was associated with lower competence, relatedness and autonomy (Evans, McPherson, Davidson 2012). [S]
- **Teaching style:** among 213 university music students, autonomy-supportive teaching predicted autonomous motivation, which predicted practice time and quality; controlling teaching did not predict either (Bonneville-Roussy and Evans 2024). [S] This is a university sample; extrapolating to children is my judgment [K].
- **Practice quality and spacing:** distributed practice (spaced study) beats massed study broadly, and the best gap grows with how long you need to retain the material (Cepeda et al. 2006 meta-analysis, verbal recall; skill-learning reviews cited inside it are mirror tracing and video-game acquisition, not music). [S] It supports "little and often" and spaced check-ins, but is indirect for music. Deliberate practice explains about 21% of variance in music performance in a 2014 meta-analysis, so practice matters but is not everything. [S†]
- **Early-stage practice:** McPherson (2005) argues amount of practice alone is an inadequate account of early development; mental strategies matter. [S†] Hallam et al. found practice strategies grow more effective with expertise. [S†]
- **Rituals that work:** Play On Philly ends each of three sessions with a jury and performance, adds a citizenship report, instrument-family festivals and guest-artist days. [S]

### 4.2 Recommended engagement system (ideal)

| Element | Frequency | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Home practice log (paper card, or emailed form with a signed link; no accounts) | Daily entry, weekly parent glance | Habit data, teacher can spot-check |
| Monthly masterclass or check-in (45 to 60 min, groups of 6 to 10) | Monthly in Phase C | Competence, feedback, new goals |
| Short video check-in (60 to 90 seconds, emailed or uploaded through a signed link) | Monthly, 10 minutes of teacher time to review | Feedback between masterclasses; no new platform needed |
| Termly showcase (recital) | Every 12 weeks | Visible competence, relatedness |
| Termly progress review ("jury" in Play On Philly's term) | Every 12 weeks | Goal setting, Home-Ready or level-up decision |
| Ensemble day (students play together, worship-band style) | Once a term | Relatedness; applies skills to church and community music |
| Open practice night | Monthly | Optional on-site time, Phase B and C students |
| Peer pods of 3 to 4 neighbors | Weekly, self-run | Relatedness at home |
| Parent touchpoint | Each term | Keeps adult supporter engaged |

Safety net: a "step back" rule. If a student misses two consecutive check-ins or the log lapses for 3 weeks, offer a return to Phase B for one term, with seats reserved for this (see section 5). [K]

Autonomy support in practice: let the student choose one of the pieces each term, ask what they want to play, and use choice-based goals; keep teacher language informational, not controlling. [S] (principle), [K] (application).

Data reminder from the project rules: Gamo has no public accounts, so logs, reviews and video check-ins must run through staff-editable forms and signed, expiring links, with no personal data or names in email subjects.

## 5. How freeing capacity works

### 5.1 Concepts

- **Station:** one instrument position (a piano, a guitar from the site pool, a kit). A station-hour is one student using one station for an hour, in class or in supervised practice.
- **Seat:** a place in a phase. Phase A seat uses the most station-hours; Phase B about half; Phase C almost none.
- **Cohort:** students who start together at a term start. Three intakes a year at 12-week terms fit the three-session structure used by Play On Philly. [S]
- **Waitlist:** an ordered list per site and instrument; seats are offered in order of application date only, never by payment or sponsorship (mission rule). Confirm 4 weeks before term start; release a seat if a student misses the first two sessions without notice. [K]

### 5.2 Formula

Capacity per year (station-hours) = N stations x H open hours a week x u utilization x T teaching weeks.

New starts per year, steady state, is about:

```
Capacity / ( SH_A + SH_B + (years in C minus the first year) x SH_C )
```

where SH_A, SH_B and SH_C are station-hours per student for the phase (class plus supervised or open practice).

### 5.3 Worked example (piano site) [M]

Assumptions: N = 8 digital pianos; open 20 hours a week; u = 80%; T = 36 teaching weeks; class 45 minutes; piano pattern from section 2.4; Phase C costs 9 station-hours a year (monthly 60-minute masterclass); ensemble days use a church hall, not stations; students stay in C for 2 further years.

- Capacity per week = 8 x 20 x 0.8 = 128 station-hours; per year = 128 x 36 = 4,608.
- Per student, year 1: Phase A 36 station-hours (12 class + 24 supervised); Phase B 20.5 (10.5 class + 10 open); total 56.5. Years 2 and 3: 9 each.
- **Baseline (every student keeps class plus one supervised practice forever):** 1.75 station-hours a week x 36 = 63 a year. Roster = 4,608 / 63 = **about 73 students in total**, with only about 24 new starts a year if tenure averages 3 years.
- **With graduation:** steady-state average 4,608 / (56.5 + 2 x 9) = about 62 new starts a year. A week-by-week simulation checking the peak (not just the average) allows 3 intakes of 18 = **54 new students a year**, 4 intakes of 14 = 56, 6 intakes of 9 = 54, with peak load about 120 to 125 of 128 station-hours. Roster after 3 years: about 54 x 3 = **160 learners** on the same 8 pianos (before attrition).
- **If Phase A used only 1 supervised practice a week** (the current model), the same simulation allows about 63 new starts a year (3 intakes of 21) but progress should be slower and Phase A longer (my expectation [K]).
- **Scaling:** about 7 new starts per station per year. N = 4 gives about 27; N = 12 about 81; N = 20 about 135.

Model limits: ignores no-shows, attrition, loan logistics and teacher capacity; assumes classes and open practice are scheduled evenly across the week. Teacher and supervisor hours are separate constraints: Phase A needs supervised-practice monitors (a ratio and screening decision for the child-safety research file).

### 5.4 Home instruments are the new constraint

At steady state about 108 students sit in Phase C at once (54 a year x 2 years) plus about 54 in B. If a share *s* needs a loan, with average loan length *L* years:

Lending pool needed = new starts per year x s x L.

Example: 54 x 30% x 2 years = about 32 digital pianos; at $450 to $700 each with pedal and stand, about $15,000 to $22,000 *(unverified; [M] from [S] prices)*. The funding source is an owner decision.

## Implications for Gamo Academy

Concrete numbers (all [M] or [K] unless marked; validate in a pilot site):

1. **Term and year:** 12-week terms, 3 intakes a year, 36 teaching weeks, a first commitment of one year (3 terms), with a showcase and a progress review at the end of every term. Basis: Play On Philly three sessions each with a jury [S], WCET 38 weeks and one-year minimum [S], RCM adult 28-week levels [S].
2. **Class and practice structure:** 45-minute weekly class; supervised practice 2 a week for the first 8 weeks, then 1 a week through the end of Phase A.
3. **Home-ready points:** piano week 16 (range 12 to 24); guitar and bass week 12 (range 8 to 16); drums week 16 for the kit, pad from week 1; voice nothing to take home, home practice from week 1.
4. **Phase B and C rhythm:** B is weekly class for 8 weeks then every other week, open practice every other week, home about 100 minutes a week. C is a monthly 60-minute masterclass, one ensemble day a term, a short video review monthly, home about 120 minutes a week.
5. **Hours by end of year 1:** about 90 to 105 total learning hours (piano about 90); 19 to 23 of them class time; about 22 to 34 supervised or open on-site practice; about 33 to 47 at home (drums add about 16 pad hours in Phase A).
6. **Capacity:** 8 stations, 20 open hours a week, 80% utilization gives 128 station-hours a week and about 54 new students a year (3 intakes of 18), a roster of about 160 by year 3 compared with about 73 if no one ever graduates. About 7 new starts per station per year.
7. **Seat planning:** reserve 10% of seats (about 2 per intake of 18) for "step back" returns [K]; waitlist ordered by application date only; confirm 4 weeks before term; release a seat after two missed starting sessions.
8. **Home instruments:** target a lending pool of about 30 weighted digital pianos at 54 starts a year if 30% of families need a loan and loans last 2 years; entry weighted piano about $400 to $500 [S]; guitar, bass, kit and pad pools are cheaper. Use a written loan agreement (care, storage, damage terms) modeled on the Play On Philly rules [S]; do not tie a loan to fees without owner approval.
9. **Gate:** use the five-part Home-Ready Check; do not release on time alone.
10. **Tooling constraints:** practice logs, reviews, video check-ins and loan agreements run through staff-managed forms and signed expiring links, not accounts, to fit the no-accounts rule.
11. **Public copy:** keep language about outcomes ("ready for home practice after about 12 to 16 weeks") as a goal, not a promise, until the pilot produces Gamo data.

## Trade-offs when constrained

- **Only one supervised practice a week in Phase A (current model):** extend Phase A by about 4 weeks (piano 20, guitar and bass 16, drums 20, voice 12) and add short daily at-home listening or silent-fingering tasks (as Suzuki programs use daily listening [S]) on the other days; throughput rises to about 63 new starts a year in the example, but expect slower progress. [M, K]
- **Fewer stations:** move guitar, bass and drums to home sooner (cheap items), keep piano at the longest A, add a pad or silent-keyboard station to widen supervised practice, and stagger starts to six small cohorts to flatten the peak (6 intakes of 9 gave the same capacity as 3 of 18). [M]
- **No lending pool or funds for instruments:** require the home-ready decision to include a family route (buy or retailer rent-to-own) and hold the student in Phase B-with-open-practice (more on-site hours) until one is arranged; this consumes seats, so prioritize pools for piano first. [K]
- **Limited teacher time:** keep Phase C in group masterclasses (6 to 10 students) and use 60 to 90-second videos with short written feedback; skip one-to-one check-ins except at the progress review.
- **Younger children (under about 8):** add 4 to 8 weeks to Phase A and require a parent at class and in the first half of Phase B (Suzuki and Yamaha model). [S, K]
- **No suitable home space:** voice and pad drumming still work; for piano, a headphone-capable digital piano avoids noise problems.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Student ages:** which age range does Gamo Academy serve at launch? Under-8s need a different, parent-supported path and a longer Phase A.
2. **Lending program:** may Gamo own and lend instruments (liability, insurance, damage terms, who pays for repairs and loss, and whether any family contribution is allowed)? Approval needed before any commitment.
3. **Instrument funding and vendor:** who funds the first pool, and may Gamo partner with a local retailer for rent-to-own (a new external relationship needing approval)?
4. **Supervision:** who supervises on-site practice (volunteers, paid monitors, older students) and what ratio and screening apply (child-safety research file)?
5. **Online and video check-ins:** is a platform acceptable for monthly video check-ins and lessons, or must it stay with emailed signed links (no new provider without approval)?
6. **Curriculum track:** how much should Levels 1 to 4 align to exam boards versus worship and contemporary repertoire (chords, playing by ear, charts)? Exam alignment is optional here.
7. **Site capacity:** how many stations and open hours will the first host site really have, and may a church hall be used for ensemble days?
8. **Pilot data:** will the owner approve a pilot of two to three terms to measure actual hours to first songs, practice-log reliability and home-ready timing before any public promise about timing?
9. **Summer:** run a 6 to 8-week optional summer term (extra A time, ensemble camp) or leave summers free?
10. **Level 3 and 4 exit:** at Level 3 or 4 does Gamo expect students to move into church music teams (Gamo Worship) or private teachers, and how does the Academy hand off?
