# 06 - Hosting sites and operations (Gamo Academy)

Research date: 2026-11-16. Scope: how Gamo Academy should choose, partner with, equip, staff, protect and run host sites (churches, schools and other interested organizations) in several major cities, and how to repeat that in each new city.

## Summary

- **One ideal site, defined by safety first, sound second.** A good host has a lockable teaching room with a window in the door, a second adult always in the building, a separate quiet-enough room for each simultaneous class, step-free access, an easy drop-off and a parents' waiting area. Rooms that share a wall with a sanctuary or gym fail on sound, not just comfort. Door and wall leaks, not wall thickness alone, usually defeat isolation [S: Wenger].
- **Use two documents with each host: an MOU for the relationship and a facility-use agreement for the space, plus a safeguarding addendum.** Insurers that advise churches say every outside user, even a friendly one, should sign a use agreement with a release, indemnity, certificate of insurance and additional-insured status, and that all of it should be reviewed by a local attorney [S: Insurance Board, GuideStone]. Gamo handles registration, payments, teachers and curriculum; the host handles the building. Hosts never touch tuition.
- **Child safety follows the CDC's six components**: screening, interaction guidelines, monitoring, safe environments, responding, training [S: CDC]. Background checks are one layer, not the whole defense [S: CDC]. Because families stay on site, Gamo can add a parent-on-site rule, which is a large practical safeguard.
- **Insurance is a real line item and a real gate.** Abuse and molestation (A&M) cover is typically separate from general liability and is hard and costly to get in 2026 [S: USLI, Gallagher]. Published premium examples for small youth programs range from about $815 to about $8,981 a year [S: USLI]; get broker quotes before any site opens **[BROKER]**.
- **Standard site kit.** Roughly $10,000 to $15,000 (unverified) of instruments and supplies per 48-student site, with a tracked inventory, an annual repair budget and a rotation plan between sites. Only two prices were verified in sources (a digital piano and a 3/4 classical guitar).
- **Teacher pay and Gamo's administrative allocation are always separate lines**, and pay is never reduced to fund Gamo's allocation. Published benchmarks for music-teacher hourly pay are wide (about $28 median for a broad "instructor" category to about $51 average for private teachers) [S†], so Gamo should set pay by local market data and keep it transparent.
- **Music licensing is a trap to plan for early.** A church's CCLI license covers copying music for congregational singing, not performing music [S: CCS]. The religious-service copyright exemption is narrow and probably does not cover a student recital; a performance license (for example one bundling ASCAP, BMI and SESAC) may be needed, and a host's license may not cover Gamo events **[COUNSEL]**.
- **Repeatable launch.** A 12-week launch calendar, a site kit, a city scorecard and a host scorecard, and per-site metrics (enrollment, attendance, retention, waitlist, satisfaction). Partner payment or sponsorship never changes vetting, scoring or rankings.

## 1. What makes a good host site

### 1.1 Principles

1. **Safety and supervision first.** CDC guidance says to control who is present, to know where youth are at all times, to define when the organization is responsible for youth, and to be especially careful in multi-organization facilities [S: CDC]. A shared building (church, school) is exactly that situation.
2. **Sound isolation second.** Several classes plus a waiting room in one building fail quickly if guitars bleed into piano lessons. Wenger's acoustics guide (a music-facility vendor, so treat as practice guidance, not a neutral standard) says leaks around doors, ducts and outlets often defeat an otherwise good wall; walls should reach the roof deck and be sealed at floor and ceiling; sound-rated doors should be STC 43 minimum; STC ratings do not simply add; and shared HVAC ducts can carry sound between rooms [S: Wenger].
3. **Families stay close.** Students arrive with a family member who drives. So parking, a safe drop-off line and a pleasant waiting area matter as much as the classroom. Choose sites near where families already are (home, school, church) so the drive is short [K].
4. **Ready to scale down to nothing.** The site must be able to be cleared and locked in 15 minutes so the host's own programs are not disturbed [K].

### 1.2 Room count and size (ideal)

| Site tier | Enrolled students | Simultaneous learners | Teaching rooms | Other rooms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | up to 24 | up to 8 | 1 | waiting area, locked storage |
| Standard (default design target) | up to 48 | up to 16 | 2 (one keyboard room, one guitar/percussion room) | flex/ensemble room (can be a hall), waiting area, locked storage |
| Large | 72 or more | up to 24 | 3 | ensemble room, practice nooks, office or desk for the coordinator |

Sizing rules of thumb (all (unverified) judgment, to be checked by a music-facility architect or the Wenger planning guides): about 25 to 35 square feet per student in a group room with instruments; ceiling height of at least 9 feet; a rectangular room avoids flutter echoes; no room smaller than about 120 square feet for a teacher plus two students if a one-on-one or pair lesson is ever offered [K].

### 1.3 Environment checklist notes

- **Sound isolation.** No shared wall with a sanctuary, gym or kitchen where possible; solid-core door with perimeter seals and a drop seal; no louvered doors; walls sealed to the deck; avoid shared return-air ducts between teaching rooms (crosstalk). A door window gives supervision, so use a sound-rated vision panel rather than a plain hollow door [S: Wenger for doors, seals, ducts; K for the vision-panel pairing].
- **Interior sound.** Carpet or area rugs and some soft surfaces reduce echo; drums are the loudest instrument, so start with practice pads and an electronic kit, or hand percussion, and use headphones on keyboards [K].
- **Floors.** Level, non-slip, easy to clean; carpet in the keyboard room helps with chair noise; no cables across walkways [K].
- **Power.** At least one grounded outlet per two keyboard stations; surge-protected power strips; cord covers; no daisy-chained strips; outlet near each amp [K].
- **Storage.** A lockable closet or cabinet for instruments, sized for the kit (roughly 40 to 60 square feet for a Standard site) (unverified), with shelving for guitars and a rack for stands. Humidity and temperature stability matter for wood instruments; avoid attics, garages and basements prone to damp [K].
- **Restrooms.** Close to the teaching rooms but visible from where an adult is stationed; single-user family restrooms are best; adults never accompany a child into a multi-stall restroom alone (see section 5.2) [S: CDC, S†: church guidance on two unrelated adults].
- **Parking and drop-off.** A marked loading lane, one-way flow, a staff or volunteer greeter in a vest, no student walks through a parking lot unaccompanied, and a rule that students are handed to a greeter or released to an authorized adult only [S: CDC on admit and release; K for lane design].
- **Accessibility.** The ADA Title III exemption for religious entities is broad; a secular tenant operating a public accommodation in a church building is commonly viewed as likely covered for its own operations, but sources disagree about the landlord's exposure [S†: ADA National Network material, DOJ preamble, a state government page] **[COUNSEL]**. Ideal for Gamo: require a step-free route to the teaching room, an accessible restroom, a seated option for every instrument position and accessible parking, whichever way the law falls [K].
- **Kitchen and waiting area for parents.** Seating for at least one family member per enrolled child in the largest session (unverified), visibility into the corridor, hot water or a kettle, and a place for siblings. Parents are welcome hosts, not supervisors. Hospitality must not feel like religious pressure on a diverse community (see 2.5) [K].
- **Wi-Fi.** Reliable guest Wi-Fi for attendance entry, tablet metronomes and backing tracks; a cellular fallback for the coordinator [K].
- **Heating and cooling.** Quiet HVAC; comfortable temperature in all seasons; a plan for hot days if the hall has no air conditioning [K].
- **Proximity.** A short drive for most enrolled families; measured by the drive-time map of waitlist and inquiry zip codes (see section 7.2) [K].

### 1.5 Scoring rubric (100 points)

Score each criterion 0 to 5 and multiply by the weight. Gates in 1.4 are pass or fail before scoring.

| Criterion | Weight | 0 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Safety and supervision design | 5 | Isolated room, no second adult | Vision panel, second adult usually present | Vision panel, dedicated greeter, second adult always present, sign-in desk |
| Sound isolation | 4 | Shared wall with loud space | Solid door, sealed, no shared duct | Sound-rated door and sealed walls to deck, separate HVAC returns |
| Room count and size | 3 | One small room | Fits tier requirement | Exceeds tier, flex room available |
| Accessibility | 3 | Steps only | Accessible entrance, restroom needs work | Full step-free route, accessible restroom and parking |
| Drop-off, parking, pickup flow | 2 | Street drop-off | Marked lane and greeter | Dedicated lane, one-way flow, covered waiting |
| Family proximity and demand | 2 | Demand unproven | Waitlist or inquiry signals within 20 min drive | Strong signals within 15 min drive |
| Facilities support (power, storage, Wi-Fi, HVAC) | 1 | Missing two or more | Meets all with workarounds | Meets all cleanly |

Weighted total = sum of (score x weight), maximum 100 when each weight is multiplied by 5 (20 points per weight unit). Decision bands (judgment, unverified): 80 or above, go; 65 to 79, conditional with a fix list and a 30-day re-score; under 65, do not open. Gates always override score. The rubric is deliberately not influenced by what the host pays or offers (see section 7.5).

## 2. Partnership model with a host

### 2.1 Two documents, plus an addendum

| Document | What it is | Binding? | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partnership MOU | Relationship document: shared goals, roles, communication, review dates | Usually a statement of intent; can have binding parts **[COUNSEL]** | Align on mission, respect, duties of each party, how to disagree |
| Facility-use agreement (license) | Permission to use named spaces at named times | Yes | Space, schedule, insurance, indemnity, damage, keys, cancellation |
| Safeguarding addendum | Gamo's child-safety rules for Gamo sessions | Yes, attached to the use agreement | Two-adult rule, screening, pickup release, incident reporting, no unsupervised access |

Why both. A written use agreement defines who maintains and secures the premises, who supervises invitees and who insures what; insurers that serve churches say outside users, even friendly ones with a compatible mission, should be treated at arm's length and sign one, and that the agreement should be reviewed by a local attorney [S: Insurance Board]. GuideStone recommends a written application per outside group, an indemnity waiver, proof of insurance and additional-insured status, written incident-reporting procedures and one assigned person to handle facility-use requests [S: GuideStone]. A friendly MOU alone does not carry that risk transfer. A facility-use agreement alone is cold and says nothing about the relationship. Using both is the most recommended pattern in the sources.

### 2.2 Who provides what

| Area | Gamo provides | Host provides |
|---|---|---|
| Registration, waitlists, enrollment | Yes (forms and emails) | Nothing; may share information about the program with its own community |
| Tuition, invoices, refunds, hardship pathway | Yes. No money changes hands at the host | Nothing |
| Teachers and assistants | Yes: hiring, vetting, training, pay | Optionally a screened volunteer greeter |
| Curriculum and music licensing | Yes | Informs Gamo which licenses the host holds |
| Instruments and equipment | Yes (site kit) | Locked storage space |
| Space | Pays nothing or a cost-recovery contribution only if the owner approves it | Named rooms, set times, utilities, cleaning of common areas |
| Insurance | Gamo's liability, A&M and accident cover for its activities | Host's property and liability cover for the building; additional-insured exchange both ways |
| Safeguarding | Policy, training, incident reports, pickup control | Cooperation, key control, second adult if arranged, reporting to Gamo within 24 hours of any incident |
| Communications | Parent emails, schedule changes, weather notices | A named contact; building-closure notices to Gamo's coordinator at least a stated time before sessions |
| Branding | Gamo identity and approved wording | Host name on location line only; no host logo on Gamo forms unless approved |

All fees, rent, utility contributions and tiers are proposals until approved by the owner. Gamo does not accept host money or sponsorship in exchange for vetting or ranking outcomes (section 7.5).

1. Parties, effective date, authority to sign
2. Description of premises (named rooms, common areas, storage, restrooms, parking)
3. Permitted use and schedule; blackout dates; holiday calendar
4. Fees or cost-recovery contribution (only if approved), deposits, payment terms
5. Insurance requirements: liability limits, medical payments, additional-insured status, certificates of insurance, notice of cancellation
6. Release, indemnification and defense (mutual where possible) **[COUNSEL]**
7. Child and vulnerable-person protection (attach addendum; host's policy for host activities)
8. Supervision, access control, keys and alarms

10. Condition of premises; damage and repair; cleaning

12. Incident reporting and emergency procedures

14. Communications and contacts
15. Signage, branding and photography

18. Term, renewal, termination for cause and for convenience
19. Dispute resolution, governing law
20. Signatures and exhibits (floor plan, schedule, certificate forms, safeguarding addendum)

Typical content in real church-facility agreements matches this: a license to use rather than a lease, hold-harmless and indemnification, insurance proof, and a statement that the church provides no supervision of the outside group [S†: Church Law & Tax, UCC, Brotherhood Mutual, Resurrection Church samples]. A Brotherhood Mutual sample reportedly makes the outside user solely responsible for screening and supervising children at its function [S†]. The Insurance Board says a minimum of a $1 million liability limit with at least $5,000 of medical payments, additional-insured status and a certificate of insurance are typical asks [S]. Its low-cost tenant liability program is typically not available for youth activities [S], so Gamo should assume it needs its own policy.

### 2.5 Religious neutrality and inclusion language (for a Christian-mission academy serving a diverse community)

Gamo is a Christian, mission-driven Public Benefit Corporation. Hosts may be churches, schools or other organizations. Families of any background must be able to enroll without feeling pressured. Suggested principles (all **[COUNSEL]** and owner review before use):

- **Open to all.** Gamo Academy teaches music to any child whose family wants to enroll, regardless of faith or no faith.
- **Instruction is music instruction.** Lessons, recitals and communications are not worship services. Any optional prayer, blessing or devotional moment is announced in advance, clearly optional, and never connected to placement, grading, scholarship or access.
- **Host activities stay the host's.** The host does not require students or parents to attend host services, give host contact details or take part in host events.
- **Teachers are not asked to proselytize** and are asked not to ask students about their beliefs. Teacher hiring practices that consider religion raise legal questions for a for-profit company and vary by state **[COUNSEL]**.
- **Repertoire** is chosen for musical learning, with hymns, worship songs and secular music all available; the choice is explained to families in the program handbook.
- **If federal funds are ever involved,** equal-treatment rules generally require that religious activity be voluntary and separate in time or place from the funded service, with notice to participants [S†: ACF and DOJ summaries]; the rules have changed over administrations, so check current text **[COUNSEL]**. This is a useful model even without federal money.

Gamo's wording rules apply: no "nonprofit", tax-exempt, charity or donation language anywhere in host communications; approved wording is Christian, mission-driven Public Benefit Corporation (`AGENTS.md`).

### 3.2 Care, maintenance and replacement planning

- **Asset tags and a ledger.** Every instrument gets a numbered tag, a photo, a purchase date and a condition grade (A new, B good, C worn, D retire). Keep the ledger in Gamo's admin (a staff-only inventory) rather than a public tool. Log location by site and date [K].
- **Condition checks.** A 10-minute check at the start and end of each term: strings, tuning stability, key function, cracks, straps, cables. Keyboard room is checked for sticky keys and spilled drinks [K].
- **Replacement reserve.** Budget a yearly repair and replacement reserve of roughly 8% to 12% of inventory value (unverified heuristic) and review after the first two years with real data. Digital pianos often last 7 to 10 years and guitars longer with care, but children's use is hard on small parts (unverified) [K].
- **Environment.** Avoid temperature extremes; do not leave guitars in cars; keep humidity moderate; no food or drink in the instrument room [K].
- **Lost or damaged instruments.** The loan agreement says what normal wear is, how to report damage and what happens if an instrument is lost. Replacement charges to families are a financial term that needs owner approval and a hardship waiver so that cost never keeps a child out of music **[COUNSEL]**.

### 3.3 Sharing and rotating inventory across sites

- **Core kit stays; flex kit rotates.** Each site owns its core (keyboards, guitars, stands). A small flex pool (extra guitars of small sizes, amps, a kit) moves between sites by term, according to enrollment growth and instrument demand [K].
- **Regional depot.** One climate-controlled space per city (a staff member's home office or a host with spare room) for spares, repairs, strings, and instruments in transit [K].
- **Instrument mix follows demand.** Use enrollment forms and waitlist data to set the next term's mix; do not buy for a guess [K].
- **Small sizes matter.** Children outgrow 1/2 and 3/4 guitars quickly, which makes loaner pools more useful than purchases; plan a growth ladder [K].
- **Take-home loans** use a loan agreement with a condition checklist at issue and return, photos, a return date, and a parent signature by a signed email link, not an account (see 6.9).

## 4. Staffing at a site

### 4.1 Roles and ratios

| Role | Who | Main duties | Ratio (ideal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site coordinator | Gamo-employed, adult, screened | Opens and closes the site, runs sign-in and pickup, host liaison, attendance, incident reports, supplies, emergency lead | 1 per site every session; may cover 2 sites in the first term (see section 10) |
| Lead teacher | Gamo-employed or engaged, adult, screened and observed | Plans and teaches, sets cohort goals, communicates progress | 1 per cohort of no more than 6 for guitar or similar; no more than 8 for headphone keyboards with an assistant |
| Assistant or mentor | Adult 18 or older, screened | Helps with tuning, logistics, restroom runs, small-group practice | 1 per 8 students in a room, plus ensures a second adult in every teaching room |
| Volunteer greeter | Adult, screened, trained | Doors, drop-off line, wayfinding, welcoming families | 1 per entrance and 1 per drop-off lane during peak |
| Host liaison | Host staff | Keys, building issues, host calendar | 1 named person |

Evidence on ratios.
- The CDC says there is no standard ratio for all situations and tells organizations to consider age, risk of the activity and location, and to require adults to actively interact with youth rather than chat in a corner [S: CDC].
- Small-group instrumental programs commonly run 3 to 6 students per teacher and no authoritative music-education standard was found [S†: UK council guidance and studio programs].
- Church children's ministry guidance varies; for elementary-age children one handbook lists 2 adults per 10 to 12 children, and sources recommend at least two unrelated adults and background-checked workers 18 or older [S†: church handbooks, UMC Discipleship].
- Across ESUSA programs (about 24,916 students and over 6,000 teachers) the average is roughly four students per teacher; this is a network average, not a site standard [S: ESUSA census; the ratio is my arithmetic].
- Gamo's design target (unverified): instructional ratio of 6:1 (guitar, percussion), 8:1 (keyboard lab with assistant), and always two unrelated screened adults in the building, with at least one adult for every room that has children in it [K].

Teenage helpers: allowed only as junior mentors who never count toward the adult total, never alone with a child, and never in restrooms, consistent with church guidance that teens do not count toward the adult number [S†].

### 4.3 Teacher hiring and vetting

CDC steps that apply directly [S: CDC]:
- Define criteria first, document how screening information will be used, and apply it consistently; talk to an attorney so screening does not violate employment law.
- Use a written application, an in-person interview with child-safety questions, and **verbal references** matched to employment history.
- Get permission before a criminal-records check, and run it near the end of the process to save cost.
- Choose the type and level of check (name, fingerprint, sex-offender registry, county, state, national) and recognize that records are not linked or complete; applicants who moved often may need multi-state checks.
- Criminal checks miss most offenders because most were never caught; use them as one layer, not the defense [S: CDC].
- Keep a record of disqualified applicants and dismissed workers so they cannot reapply [S: CDC].

Gamo additions (all (unverified) judgment):
- A **paid trial lesson** with an observer, not a free audition.
- At least two references, one from a teaching or youth-work context.
- A written code of conduct with plain rules on one-on-one contact, social media, gifts, transport and private contact with students.
- A probation period of 90 days with classroom observations by the lead or a Gamo staff member.
- Re-screening cycle: every 3 years at the longest (a UMC resource uses three years for background checks [S†]); annual self-disclosure of any new offense or investigation.
- Typical per-check vendor costs of about $15 to $40 are quoted in vendor comparison blogs [S†]; confirm with vendors.

## 5. Safeguarding and risk

### 5.1 Child-safety policy contents (ideal)

Structure the policy around the CDC's six components [S: CDC], with content from CDC and church-insurer guidance [S: Church Mutual].

1. **Screening and selection.** Application, interviews, verbal references, background checks, permission forms, written criteria, disqualification list.
2. **Guidelines on interactions.** Appropriate and inappropriate behavior with examples; limit one-on-one contact by having at least two adults present; choose one of three CDC options: mandatory always, risk-based, or extra safeguards if the mission requires one-on-one time (for example mentoring) [S: CDC]. Church Mutual states the plainest version: at least two adults whenever children are involved, and the same rule applies to online contact such as email and video, with parent copied [S: Church Mutual]. Darkness to Light frames the rule as limiting opportunities for one adult to be alone with one child [S†].
3. **Monitoring behavior.** Observation and supervision, ratios, supervisors who walk through, peer-to-peer rules (bullying, sexual behavior between youth) [S: CDC].
4. **Safe environments.** Bright lighting; no hidden rooms; visibility; access control by monitoring who is present; admit and release procedures; privacy when toileting; defining where Gamo's responsibility begins and ends, especially in a shared building [S: CDC].
5. **Responding.** How to report a concern, to whom (a named safeguarding lead, then the state agency), how to respond to breaches, allegations, and press and community, with a clear no-retaliation rule [S: CDC].
6. **Training.** See 4.2.

Add: a policy on photography, social media and private messaging; a lost-child procedure; a procedure for adults arriving impaired.

### 5.2 Supervision rules

- No adult is ever alone with one child in a closed or unobservable space. Teaching room doors have a window or stay open [S: CDC for principle, K for door rule].
- Two unrelated screened adults in the building at all times children are present.
- Restroom trips: students go in pairs or with an adult stationed outside; no adult enters a multi-stall restroom alone with children; younger children are accompanied by a parent [S†: church guidance; K].
- Families stay on site or return before the exact end time. Gamo's responsibility begins at sign-in and ends at sign-out.
- No private transport of students by staff or volunteers; no meeting students privately; no gifts without parent knowledge [K].

### 5.3 Pickup release procedure

Pattern observed in practice [S: YMCA of Greater Toronto; S†: other youth-program forms]:
1. At enrollment the parent or guardian lists authorized adults (names, relationship, phone); minimum age for a pickup adult is set by policy (a YMCA policy treats a responsible person 16 or older as an adult for arrival and departure [S], a university policy requires 18 [S†]; Gamo sets 18 (unverified)).
2. Release only to a parent, guardian or listed authorized adult.
3. If staff do not know the person, a second staff member confirms the person is on the list; if not possible, check government-issued photo ID against the list [S].
4. Never release to an unlisted person, including relatives, without direct confirmation from the parent through a verified channel [S†: Mountaineers form].
5. Late pickup: call the parent, then the emergency contact, notify the supervisor; if there is still no contact after a set time (the YMCA policy uses one hour) call the regional child protection agency and follow its instructions [S].
6. Changes to the authorized list arrive by a signed email link or form (no accounts), recorded with a time stamp, at least 24 hours before pickup unless the parent calls the coordinator [S† for the 24-hour pattern].
7. A student who has not arrived: check with the parent and record in the incident log [S: YMCA].

Gamo implementation (see section 11): collect the list in an enrollment form; show it to the coordinator on the day's roster in admin; sign-out recorded by staff; stored under the retention rules.

### 5.4 Incident reports

Required for any injury, illness, behavior event, safeguarding concern, property damage, near-miss, late or unauthorized pickup, or complaint. Written the same day by staff who saw it; facts only; no names in email subjects; parent notified the same day for any injury or concern; safeguarding concerns go straight to the safeguarding lead and, where required, to the state agency; host notified of incidents on its property within 24 hours (GuideStone recommends written reporting procedures and immediate reporting to a designated host representative [S: GuideStone]). Incident reports are staff-only records with limited access and retention rules.

### 5.5 Medical and allergy information

Enrollment form collects emergency contacts, allergies (food, environmental, medication), medical conditions relevant to the class, any accommodations (for example sensory needs), and consent for emergency care. The coordinator has a printed or screen roster with allergies. No food in teaching rooms; snacks only in the waiting area; a nut-aware rule for shared snacks (unverified). The site has a first-aid kit; whether to stock an epinephrine auto-injector and who may use it depends on state law and training **[COUNSEL]**. Medication administration by staff is not offered unless approved and trained [K].

### 5.6 Photo and video consent

Opt-in, per purpose, revocable: (a) internal use; (b) Gamo public photos; (c) host-community use. Default no photos of faces without consent. Parents may photograph their own child at recitals under a published recital rule; no posting of other children's images by Gamo without consent. Children's names are not published with photos. Staff do not use personal phones for photos of students [K].

### 5.7 Emergency procedures

Fire evacuation with posted routes and a head count against the roster; severe weather shelter in an interior room; lockdown or shelter-in-place; medical emergency with 911 and a named responder; missing child; reunification plan with parents at a known place; host building's own plan reviewed and adopted for Gamo times; practice drills each term; an emergency card with host address, host contact and nearest hospital [K].

## 6. Daily and weekly operations

### 6.1 Cohorts and scheduling

- **Cohort.** 6 to 8 students of similar age and level, same instrument, the same teacher, the same day and time for a 10-to-12 week term (unverified proposal) [K]. The ESUSA average is about 5 hours a week across grades and about 7 hours for elementary and middle school [S: ESUSA]; Gamo's model is lighter at one or two sessions a week and can grow.
- **Session shape.** A 75 to 90-minute block with a short arrival buffer, 20 to 25 minutes of group technique, 20 to 25 minutes of rotation or pair practice, 10 to 15 minutes of ensemble play-along, and a 5-minute dismissal [K].
- **Term calendar.** Fixed start and end dates, holiday breaks announced at enrollment, and a recital in the final week [K].
- **Overlap rules.** No class begins while another is finishing in the same corridor; stagger arrivals by 10 minutes to protect the drop-off lane [K].
- **Weekly rhythm.** Teachers arrive 30 minutes early; coordinator 45 minutes early for set-up, checks and host handshake; 30-minute reset after the last class (stand and chair counts, lock-up) [K].

### 6.2 Attendance

- Attendance is taken at every session by the coordinator or lead teacher in admin, with arrival and sign-out times and the adult who signed the child out.
- Absence follow-up by email the same day; two consecutive absences trigger a personal contact (phone or email from the coordinator).
- Attendance data feeds retention and progress metrics (see 7.4). Retention is the main signal programs use: the Community Music Works evaluation found about 90% retention and noted students lose ground when they do not feel progress on their instrument; engagement supports such as practice buddies, outside performances and progress benchmarks help [S: CMW]. Industry blogs quote 75% to 85% retention as typical, which is low-reliability [S†].

### 6.3 Make-up policy

Teacher illness or site closure: Gamo makes up the session (a replacement session at the same site, a combined session or a credit). Student absence: one make-up per term at another cohort's session if a seat is open, or a take-home practice packet; otherwise no make-up. A published rule, applied consistently, with hardship flexibility. Because tuition and refunds are financial terms, owner approval is needed for the final policy **[COUNSEL]**.

### 6.4 Weather and cancellation communications

- Decision made by 2 hours before the first session; decision-maker is the coordinator with the lead teacher; host consulted; if schools close, Gamo's default is also to close (unverified).
- Notice goes out by email (the outbox with retries) and an on-site sign at the door; no account is needed. Subjects never include names or personal data. Optionally a text-message channel only if the owner approves a new provider (not in scope without approval).
- Messages say what is canceled, when the make-up is, and who to contact.

### 6.5 Parent communications (no public accounts)

Gamo has no public sign-in, so everything runs through forms, email and signed expiring single-purpose links (`AGENTS.md`). Needed messages: confirmation of enrollment and waitlist status; first-day information (arrival, pickup, what to bring, safety rules); schedule reminders; weather notices; progress notes each term; recital information; instrument loan and return notices; incident follow-ups; invoice or payment notices from Gamo; a manage link for authorized adult changes; end-of-term survey. Parents receive a single plain-language handbook per site.

### 6.6 Registration and waitlists

- A public registration form per site and term collects child, guardian, instrument preference, level, allergy and medical information, authorized adults, photo consent, handbook acknowledgment and agreement to the safety rules.
- Capacity is the number of seats by cohort; the form shows open, waitlist or full.
- Waitlist rules: first by registration time within level and instrument; seats offered by email with a 48-hour hold (unverified); each offer is a one-purpose signed link; waitlist demand by zip code is recorded to guide the next site and instrument purchases (section 7).
- Payment and hardship: Gamo invoices the family; no cash at the host; a hardship pathway is available and does not change placement (financial terms need owner approval).

### 6.7 Recitals and showcases

- **Venue.** The host's sanctuary or hall is the natural venue for a first recital; it needs capacity, accessible seating, a piano or digital piano with amplification, restrooms and parking. Parent-recording rules and photo consent apply (5.6).
- **Performance licensing.** The religious-service exemption in the US Copyright Act is narrow: it covers religious works performed in the course of a service at a place of worship; outside services, such as fellowship events, you probably need a performance license; a recital is not clearly a "service" [S†: Church Law & Tax, Emory Libraries, UMC Discipleship]. A separate exemption for face-to-face teaching in a nonprofit educational institution's classroom is mentioned [S†: Texas city attorneys presentation], but Gamo is a for-profit Public Benefit Corporation, so it should not rely on it without counsel **[COUNSEL]**.
- **CCLI.** CCLI's church license covers copying lyrics and music for congregational singing; it does not include the right to play or perform music in church facilities. The performance license from the same vendor family (PERFORMmusic) is for performance [S: CCS help article]; it is described as covering ASCAP, BMI and SESAC repertoire [S†]. A license held by a host generally covers the licensee's premises under its legal and financial umbrella [S†]; because Gamo is a separate legal entity, assume the host's license does not cover Gamo events until the licensor confirms in writing (unverified) **[COUNSEL]**.
- **Streaming.** Streaming or recording a recital needs separate licensing; no streaming of other people's songs without it [S†].
- **Practical choice.** Prefer public-domain hymns and folk tunes, original student pieces and licensed arrangements for the first recitals while licensing is clarified.

### 6.8 Curriculum materials and copyright

- **Method books.** Buy a copy for each student or use a licensed digital subscription. Do not photocopy or scan purchased books for the whole class; music copying guidelines are a minimum safe harbor that do not allow whole-ensemble copying to avoid purchase [S†: NATS FAQ and school music copyright guides].
- **Photos of sheet music** count as copies [S†: NATS].
- **Uploading to shared drives or learning platforms** usually needs publisher authorization [S†].
- **Original Gamo materials** are owned by Gamo; contributions by teachers are assigned in writing [K].
- **Repertoire log** per cohort so licensing questions can be answered later [K].

### 6.9 Instrument loan agreements and condition checklists

Agreements seen across school and community programs share these parts [S†: Baltimore County, Bloomfield Hills, University of Queensland, Making Music templates]: borrower is the parent or guardian who signs; responsibility for damage beyond normal wear; prompt reporting of damage; return on demand or on leaving the program; no loan to others; a replacement-cost schedule. Gamo's checklist (headings only):
1. Instrument, tag number, serial and accessories
2. Condition at issue (photo, grade, defects)
3. Term and return date
4. Care rules (case, storage, temperature, cleaning)
5. Repair and damage reporting (no outside repair without Gamo)
6. Normal wear versus damage; replacement cost schedule (owner approval; hardship waiver)
7. Loss, theft and insurance responsibility
8. Return inspection and sign-off
9. Signature by a signed email link, date and the guardian's identity

### 7.2 Choosing cities and hosts

City signals (all [K], unverified weighting):
- **Demand.** Inquiry and waitlist volume by zip code, school music participation (many programs exist because school music is limited; 88% of ESUSA programs report filling a gap and roughly half say some or all students have access to school music, while 13 report most students do not [S: ESUSA]), number of families with school-age children within a 15-minute drive.
- **Existing programs.** Competing community music schools, school band and orchestra programs, church music programs, ESUSA or similar local programs; look for complementarity rather than duplication.
- **Host pipeline.** Number of qualified host candidates within target neighborhoods; leadership interest; pastors, principals or directors willing to sign agreements.
- **Safety of area.** Daylight pickup, lighting, public transit and traffic, local safety review; this is a gating criterion for a family driving in at dusk.
- **Teacher supply.** Local pool of vetted, qualified teachers.
- **Regulatory.** State child-care licensing exemption status, background-check rules, mandated-reporter rules, insurance availability.
- **Operations density.** A depot, 3 or more sites in a city within a manageable drive, and a city coordinator.

City scorecard (0 to 5 each; weights are judgment):

| Host pipeline quality | 20% |
| Safety and logistics | 15% |
| Teacher supply | 15% |

### 7.5 Governing partners without letting money affect vetting

- **Firewall.** Host selection, scores, safeguarding approval, teacher vetting and any rankings are decided by a Gamo staff panel using the written rubric. - **Conflict-of-interest disclosure** for staff and board members who have a role in a host organization.
- **Annual site review**: safeguarding audit (policy, training records, incidents, drills), facility condition, partner satisfaction, metrics.
- **Clear termination triggers:** a safeguarding breach, repeated schedule or building failures, host activities contradicting the agreement, loss of insurance.
- **Student continuity:** if a site closes, students are offered another site or a final session and recital.
- **Transparency:** an internal log of agreements, certificates and review dates; no names or personal data in logs.
- **No exclusive deals or paid priority.**

## 9. Later: student transportation (a bus)

Deferred. Flagged for counsel and an insurance broker. The current plan assumes a family member drives each student, so none of this is needed now. High-level considerations only:

- **Federal categories.** NHTSA's vehicle-standards rules define a school bus as a vehicle designed to carry 11 or more people including the driver and sold for transporting students to and from school or school-related events; an "activity bus" is a type of school bus, and a "multifunction school activity bus" is a school bus whose purposes do not include transporting students to and from home or bus stops; state definitions can differ, and using a bus that does not meet school-bus standards for pupil transportation can create liability [S†: NHTSA and Federal Register summaries]. Whether a small church-style van for a music program counts is state-specific **[COUNSEL]**.
- **FMCSA for faith-based and similar operators.** Regulation turns on vehicle size and capacity and on whether the transportation is for hire (any payment, including as part of a package). Operators who run for-hire interstate need operating authority and, for 15 or fewer passengers including the driver, $1.5 million financial responsibility; $5 million for 16 or more; a driver of a larger bus needs a commercial driver's license with a passenger endorsement; drivers are subject to drug and alcohol testing in those cases; unpaid private use by a church has fewer requirements; and 15-passenger vans under 10,001 pounds have limited exemptions [S: FMCSA tip sheet, January 2017]. States can be stricter [S†].
- **Drivers and screening.** Licensing, medical qualification, motor-vehicle-record checks, drug testing, training, supervision on board (a second adult) and no one-on-one rides (consistent with CDC transportation guidance, which asks organizations to define who is responsible for transporting youth and when [S: CDC]).
- **Insurance.** Commercial auto and hired and non-owned auto; Gallagher reports auto rates up 7% to 15% and that many carriers will no longer write monoline auto, requiring documented safety programs, telematics and driver management [S: Gallagher].
- **Funding sources.** Not researched; likely options are grants, partner contributions and in-kind vehicles, each subject to owner approval and to the rule that funding never affects vetting [K, unverified].
- **Standards.** Seat belts, child restraints, vehicle inspection, maintenance logs, emergency drills and written policies [K].
- **Decision gate.** Do not start before sites have run for at least two terms, demand for transport is documented and counsel and the broker have reviewed [K].

## 10. Trade-offs when constrained (after the ideal)

| Constraint | Ideal | Constrained version | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Only one room | Two or three rooms | One room with staggered cohorts and headphone keyboards; guitar and keyboard on different nights | Longer evenings; lower capacity |
